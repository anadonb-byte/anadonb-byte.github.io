On **Friday 25 September 2026** I took part in **La Noche de la Investigación**, the Aragón edition of the **European Researchers' Night**, at the **CaixaForum Zaragoza** auditorium. My slot was a micro-talk of eight minutes in the evening session, in front of a general adult audience, with the title *Los imanes del futuro: diseñando materiales magnéticos en tres dimensiones* ("The magnets of the future: designing magnetic materials in three dimensions").

## What I talked about

The talk had to get from everyday magnets to 3D nanomagnetism without a single equation, so I built it around two questions and two answers.

- **Magnets are already in your pocket.** The loudspeaker, the compass, the camera focus. And, less obviously, the hard drives that store information.
- **How small is a bit?** A magnetic force micrograph of a real hard disk, one micrometre across, showing the light and dark pattern in which the data is written. Two magnetic states, a zero and a one.
- **What we want to improve.** More information in less space, using less energy. Simply shrinking each element does not work: it also has to keep the information and let us read it back without errors.
- **First answer: a magnetic whirl.** Skyrmions — which I called *remolinos magnéticos* on stage rather than use the word cold. On the same micrograph, a 20 nm skyrmion next to a real bit, drawn to scale.
- **Second answer: leave the plane.** Everything we know how to fabricate is flat. We build ramps, horseshoes and helices instead, because the shape of the material changes its magnetism — it is not only about having more room.
- **How we make them.** Focused electron beam induced deposition: a gas injection needle, an electron beam, and a structure that grows in mid-air. 3D printing several thousand times finer than a desktop printer.

## The event

The European Researchers' Night is itself a Marie Skłodowska-Curie action, which closed a loop for me: the work I was describing came out of **3D-Sky**, the MSCA fellowship I ran at INMA from July 2024 to June 2026. The Zaragoza edition was organised by the local European Researchers' Night team with Esciencia, and the CaixaForum auditorium ran micro-talks back to back for two hours.

![On stage at CaixaForum Zaragoza](/images/news/noche-investigacion-2026-charla.jpg)

Thanks to the organising team for the coaching sessions beforehand — the advice to leave the lectern, cut the text and answer "what is it for?" changed the talk substantially.
